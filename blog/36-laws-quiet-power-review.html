The 36 Laws of Quiet Power: What I Learned Writing It

Most books about influence are books about being loud. Be more confident. Command the room. Speak with authority. After reading dozens of them, I felt like they were written for someone else entirely.

I am not a loud person. I'm methodical, observant, and I think before I speak. And yet, in rooms full of louder people, I've consistently been the one who gets trusted with real responsibility. I wanted to understand why — and write down what I found.

That's where The 36 Laws of Quiet Power came from.

Quiet power is not about suppressing yourself. It's about understanding that the most durable influence comes from depth, consistency, and genuine value — not volume.

Why 36 Laws?

I didn't start with a number. I started with observations — patterns I noticed in people around me who had enormous influence without needing to perform it. People in Mumbai's startup ecosystem, in consulting firms, in engineering teams. People who were never the loudest in the room but consistently shaped decisions. I kept a notebook for eight months. When I reviewed it, I had 36 distinct, non-overlapping principles. So the book has 36 laws.

Three Laws That Changed How I Work

LAW 07
The Power of Doing Nothing (For Now)

Not every problem needs an immediate response. The person who waits until they understand something completely before speaking carries more weight when they finally do. Urgency is often manufactured. Real power knows the difference.

LAW 14
Become the Person Everyone Needs to Call

Quiet power is not passive. It's built through years of solving specific problems so reliably that you become the obvious answer to a specific category of question. Deep expertise in one area is worth more than shallow competence in ten.

LAW 29
Let Your Work Speak First

The loudest self-promoters often have the weakest work. When your work genuinely solves a problem for someone, they tell others. Word-of-mouth from satisfied people is the most powerful marketing that exists — and it requires exactly zero performance.

What I Didn't Expect

I wrote this book expecting it to resonate with introverts. What I found was that the feedback came equally from extroverts who were exhausted by performing confidence they didn't feel. The book isn't about introversion. It's about choosing substance over performance — and that's a choice available to anyone.

How It Connects to AI and Technology

In an age where AI can generate noise at scale — content, opinions, social media posts, marketing copy — the scarcest resource becomes signal. The person with quiet depth, genuine expertise, and a reputation for being right produces signal. The AI era, counterintuitively, makes quiet power more valuable, not less. The signal-to-noise ratio of the internet is plummeting. The people who are deeply known for one real thing will rise above it.
